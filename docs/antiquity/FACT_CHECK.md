# Fact-check of the ancient-art details

Companion to [`DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md) and the other documents in this folder. This is a record of what was checked, what was changed, and what is still unchecked. **Method:** web searches in November 2026, preferring museum, encyclopedia and reference pages, and noting where sources disagree. Many search hits were Wikipedia mirrors, aggregator sites or machine-generated pages; I did not rely on those. I did not read primary texts (Pliny, Liutprand, Apollodorus) directly, only reference works that quote them, so anything that matters for a card in the gallery should still be checked against a scholarly source before release.

**Status key:** **OK** = supported by sources found · **Changed** = the document was wrong or too strong and has been edited · **Soft** = true but the sources disagree on details, so the wording is hedged · **Not checked** = I could not confirm it in this pass.

## 1. The four playable artists

| Claim in the docs | Status | Notes and sources |
|---|---|---|
| Exekias was a potter and painter, active in Athens about 545 to 530 BC, whose signature reads "Exekias made me" | **OK** | The formula is *epoiesen*, "made". Twelve of the fourteen vases carrying his name mark him as potter; two amphorae add a verse that implies he also painted. ([Wikipedia: Exekias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exekias), [Oxford CARC](https://www.carc.ox.ac.uk/carc/resources/Introduction-to-Greek-Pottery/Keypieces/blackfigure/exekias)) |
| The *Achilles and Ajax playing dice* amphora is in the Vatican; the heroes call out "three" and "four" (*tria*, *tessara*) | **OK** | Sources agree that Achilles' word is *tessara* (four) and Ajax's is *tria* (three); the spelling varies (*tesara*, *tessera*). The Vatican record lists both inscriptions. ([Oxford CARC](https://www.carc.ox.ac.uk/record/A33A92D1-A210-456B-9738-EB257BDE1306)) |
| The Dionysus cup (kylix) is in Munich, with Dionysus in a boat and dolphins | **Soft** | It is the "Dionysus Cup" (Munich, Antikensammlung 2044), dated between about 540 and 530 BC; sources differ slightly on whether the painting itself is signed. ([Wikipedia: Dionysus Cup](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Dionysus_Cup)) |
| "Tessera" comes from the Greek for "four" | **OK** | Widely stated and consistent with the inscription above (not separately re-checked in this pass). |
| Iaia of Cyzicus, a woman painter active in Rome; Pliny says she painted a self-portrait using a mirror (lost) | **OK** | Pliny *Natural History* 35.147; the Pauly-Wissowa entry places her in Rome in Varro's youth, around 100 BC. ([encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/women/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/iaia-fl-c-100-bce), [Wikipedia: Iaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iaia)) |
| Iaia was praised for her speed | **OK** | The line "no one had a quicker hand than she" is quoted in translation; her prices reportedly exceeded those of the most famous portraitists of her time. |
| Pliny's women painters are Timarete, Irene, Kalypso, Aristarete and Iaia | **Changed** | The list also includes **Olympias**, and Kalypso is disputed (she may be a subject of a painting by Irene rather than a painter). The doc now says so. None of their paintings survives. |
| Thutmose was Akhenaten's court sculptor at Amarna, and the bust of Nefertiti was found in his workshop in 1912 and is in the Neues Museum, Berlin | **OK** | Found on 6 December 1912 by a German team under Ludwig Borchardt. Berlin's labels date the bust to about 1351 to 1334 BC, so "about 1340 BC" is fair. ([SMB Berlin](https://www.smb.museum/en/exhibitions/detail/in-the-light-of-amarna/), [Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/_AWhSnxV4cRAIw)) |
| Sosus of Pergamon, a 2nd-century BC mosaicist, made *The Unswept Floor* and *The Doves Drinking*, known through Roman copies | **OK** | Pliny is the source. The Capitoline *Doves* come from Hadrian's Villa; the best-known *Unswept Floor* in the Vatican is signed by **Heraklitos**, not Sosus. ([Wikipedia: Sosus of Pergamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sosus_of_Pergamon)) |
| The *Unswept Floor* copies show a mouse | **Changed** | No source places a mouse in the Vatican copy (found on the Aventine in 1833, Hadrianic, signed by Heraklitos). The only mouse I found is in a copy from Boudry in Switzerland, nibbling a nut. The doc now says exactly that. ([Wikipedia: Asarotos oikos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asarotos_oikos)) |

| Claim | Status | Notes and sources |
|---|---|---|

| The Egyptian palette has six main colours (white, black, red, yellow, blue, green) | **OK** | ([Australian Museum](https://australian.museum/learn/cultures/international-collection/ancient-egyptian/the-painter-in-ancient-egypt/)) |

## 3. Crete and the Aegean

| Claim | Status | Notes and sources |
|---|---|---|
| The dolphin fresco is in the "Queen's Megaron" at Knossos | **Soft** | "Queen's Megaron" is Arthur Evans's name; there is no evidence it was a queen's. The doc now says so. ([Wikipedia: Minoan art](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoan_art)) |
| The bull-leaping fresco is largely reconstructed | **OK** | Most Knossos frescoes were reconstructed from fragments. |
| The Akrotiri Miniature Ship fresco (West House); the Flying Fish fresco from Phylakopi on Melos; the Palaikastro octopus flask in Heraklion | **OK** | Octopus flask about 1500 to 1450 BC. ([Smarthistory](https://smarthistory.org/?p=31724), [Wikipedia: List of Aegean frescos](https://www.Wikipedia.com/wiki/List_of_Aegean_frescos)) |
| Émile Gilliéron père (1850 to 1924) and fils (1885 to 1939) restored the Knossos frescoes for Evans | **OK (now checked)** | Both died before 1956. Some of their reconstructions, such as the "Priest-King", are widely thought to be wrong. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89mile_Gilli%C3%A9ron), [Met Museum](https://www.metmuseum.org/articles/historic-images-of-the-greek-bronze-age)) |
| The Minotaur's name was Asterion ("starry") | **OK** | ([Wikipedia: Minotaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minotaur)) |
| Talos has a single vein closed by a bronze nail at the ankle | **Soft** | That is Pseudo-Apollodorus; Apollonius gives a thin membrane instead, and other versions have Medea or an archer. The doc's "ankle plug" is the Apollodorus version. ([Theoi](https://theoi.com/Gigante/GiganteTalos.html)) |
| Iolaus cauterised the Hydra's stumps | **OK** | Pseudo-Apollodorus, *Library* 2.5.2: Iolaus sears the stumps with a burning brand. Eurystheus refused to count the labour because Heracles had help. The "two heads for one" detail is the popular version. ([Wikipedia: Lernaean Hydra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lernaean_Hydra)) |

## 4. Greece, Rome and Olympus

| Claim | Status | Notes and sources |
|---|---|---|
| Aristophanes' *The Birds* (414 BC) has Cloudcuckooland | **OK** | ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/EBchecked/topic/66528/Birds)) |
| Otus and Ephialtes (the Aloadae) piled mountains to reach Olympus | **Soft** | The usual version is Ossa on Olympus and Pelion on Ossa; ancient sources vary in the order. ([Wikipedia: Aloadae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aloadae)) |
| The Alexander Mosaic is from the House of the Faun, about 100 BC, now in Naples, after a Greek painting possibly by Philoxenos of Eretria | **OK** | The version in the house is a modern copy (2005). ([Pompeii Online](https://www.pompeionline.net/en/archaeological-park-of-pompeii/mosaic)) |
| The *Cave canem* mosaic is at the House of the Tragic Poet, Pompeii | **OK** | Dated 1st century BC (some say 2nd century BC to 1st century AD). |
| The Villa of the Mysteries has red walls and a Dionysian fresco cycle | **Soft** | Dated 2nd to 1st century BC; the red is partly cinnabar. |
| The Nile Mosaic of Palestrina is Roman, about 100 BC | **Soft** | Scholars place it between about 100 BC and Hadrian's time; "late 2nd or early 1st century BC" is the common view. (The doc does not give a date.) |
| The Colossus of Nero gave the Colosseum its name and stood nearby | **Soft** | The usual explanation, but the statue's height is given as 30 to 35 m. The doc now says "about 30 to 35 m". |
| The Colosseum had a hypogeum with lifts | **OK** | Added by Domitian; a reconstructed animal lift was tested in 2015. |
| Diogenes lived in a *pithos*, and asked Alexander to "stand a little out of my sun" | **OK, with caution** | The anecdote is in Plutarch (*Alexander* 14), but historians doubt it happened. ([Plutarch on Perseus](https://cts.perseids.org/read/greekLit/tlg0007/tlg047/perseus-eng2/12.1-14.2)) |
| The Pergamon Altar's Gigantomachy (now in Berlin), under Eumenes II | **OK** | Dated about 170 to 159 BC (some say 166 to 156). The snake-legged giants are standard but were not confirmed by this search. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pergamon_Altar)) |
| The geese that saved the Capitol | **Soft** | The traditional date is 390 BC (modern scholars say 387 BC), and historians treat the story as legend. The doc now says so. |
| The Aqua Claudia (begun by Caligula about AD 38, finished by Claudius in AD 52) and the Cloaca Maxima (traditionally 6th century BC, attributed to a Tarquin) | **OK** | ([Wikipedia: Aqua Claudia](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Aqua_Claudia), [Engineering Rome](https://engineeringrome.org/cloaca-maxima-ancient-romes-best-bargain-investment/)) |

## 5. Etruria

| Claim | Status | Notes and sources |
|---|---|---|
| The Tarquinia tombs: Leopards (about 480 to 450 BC), Triclinium (about 470 BC), Hunting and Fishing, Orcus | **OK** | The Tomb of Orcus has two parts (Orcus I, 470 to 450 BC; Orcus II, about 325 BC). The Hunting and Fishing date varies by source (late 6th century BC). ([Wikipedia: Tomb of Orcus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Orcus)) |
| Aita (Hades) and Phersipnai (Persephone) appear in the Tomb of Orcus | **OK** | In Orcus II, on the back wall. |
| Aita wears a wolf-skin cap | **Soft** | He is "rarely depicted" but "sometimes wears a wolf cap", borrowed from the older wolf deity Calu. The doc now says "sometimes". ([Wikipedia: Aita](https://kaweah.freedombox.rocks/kiwix/wikipedia_en_all_maxi_2023-10/Aita)) |
| Charun is an Etruscan death-demon with a hammer, and blue | **Changed** | The hammer is his main attribute. His colouring is described variously as bluish, grey-green or blue-grey, so the doc now says "bluish-grey". ([Wikipedia: Charun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charun)) |
| "False doors" in the Tomb of Orcus | **Changed** | Not recorded in the sources I found; the doc now calls this **our invention**. The same tomb contains something real and fun that we could use: **Tuchulcha**, a snake-haired demon, shown watching the hero Theseus play a board game. |

## 6. Byzantium and Late Antiquity

| Claim | Status | Notes and sources |
|---|---|---|
| The Throne of Solomon at the Magnaura had lions that roared and birds that sang; Liutprand of Cremona saw it in 949 | **OK** | Liutprand's *Antapodosis* describes roaring lions and a gilded tree with birds, and the throne that rose; some sources credit the mechanisms to the 9th-century emperor Theophilos. ([Wikipedia: Magnaura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnaura)) |
| Byzantine iconoclasm was 726 to 843 | **Soft** | The traditional dates. The first phase ended in 787, and the second ran from about 814 or 815 to 843 (or 842). The doc now says "traditionally, in two phases". ([encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/environment/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/iconoclasm-iconoclasm-byzantine-tradition)) |
| San Vitale, Ravenna, was consecrated in 547 | **OK** | Neither Justinian nor Theodora ever visited Ravenna. ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Church-of-San-Vitale), [Smarthistory](https://smarthistory.org/?p=1114)) |
| The Mausoleum of Galla Placidia has a starry vault | **OK** | Early 5th century (about 425 to 450): gold stars in rings on dark blue, with a gold cross at the centre. A single abstract gives 567 stars; treat as approximate. ([Medieval Mosaics](https://medievalmosaics.com/mosaics/39)) |
| Hagia Sophia was dedicated in 537 and has a ring of dome windows | **OK** | Dedicated 27 December 537; about forty windows at the base of the dome. The first dome partly collapsed in 557 and was rebuilt in 558 to 562, so the dome we see is the second. ([World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/Hagia_Sophia)) |
| Fayum portraits are Roman Egypt, 1st to 3rd century AD, in encaustic and tempera | **OK** | Some sources push the start into the 1st century BC. ([Met Museum](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2000/mummy-portraits)) |

## 8. Summary of edits made to `DESIGN.md`

1. Iaia is "active in Rome around 100 BC"; Pliny's list of women painters now includes Olympias and a disputed Kalypso.
2. The *Unswept Floor* and *Doves* are known only through Roman copies; the mouse is now tied to one copy (Boudry), not the Vatican one.
3. The word "vandalism" is "popularised" in 1794, with a note that the Vandals were probably not unusually destructive; iconoclasm is "traditionally 726 to 843, in two phases".
4. The Colossus of Nero is "about 30 to 35 m tall, sources differ".
5. Charun is bluish-grey; Aita's wolf-skin cap is "sometimes"; the false doors are called our invention.
6. The Seikilos epitaph is "probably" 1st century AD.
7. The Gilliéron dates are now checked, with a note that some of their reconstructions are disputed.
8. The Dolphin Hall note says that "Queen's Megaron" is Evans's name.
9. (Second pass) The mouse is tied to the Boudry copy; "tessera" is "usually derived" from the Greek for four; the Capitoline geese are called a traditional story.
